TIDE TABLE, computed for the Anchorage
of the Island. By A. SMOLLETT, Master
of the Schooner HISPANIOLA.

High Water at full and change of the Moon:
   Morning tide . . . . half after five.
   Evening tide . . . . half after six.

The EBB sets strong to the S.W., and runs
full three hours after the turn. In the
southern channel it runs like a mill-race,
and no boat is to leave the ship upon the
ebb without a warp to haul her back by.

The FLOOD is slack, and sets fair into the
anchorage from the N.E.

NOTE. — The current off the south point will
take a light boat and carry her round the
island faster than any man can row against
it. I have seen the like off this coast, and
warn all hands accordingly. Let no man trust
himself to a cockle-shell upon this ebb.

A.S.

[Added later, in a young hand, below:]

He was right about the ebb. — J.H.
