[A scrawled diagram on a scrap of ship's paper, in Jim Hawkins's
boyish hand. At the top a rough outline of the island's west coast,
with arrows showing the set of the currents; below, his notes.]

                    THE WEST COAST, as I found it

   [Sketch: a coastline running north & south. An arrow along the
   whole west side marked "the current sets NORTHWARD here, as
   Silver said." At the southern end: "Haulbowline Head — cliffs
   40 or 50 foot, fallen rock, breakers spout & bellow — NO
   landing." Beside it: "Mizzenmast Hill, bare & dark." Further
   north: "Cape of the Woods — so mark'd upon the chart — tall
   pines to the sea's margin — the kindlier landing." In the
   offing: "monsters here — soft snails of incredible bigness,
   barking like dogs upon the rocks (sea lions, as I have since
   understood — harmless)." A second arrow, offshore, marked
   "the ebb bears SOUTHWARD — coracle & schooner at an equal
   rate." A small schooner drawn stern-on: "HISPANIOLA under
   main-sail & two jibs, no soul steering — falls into the
   wind's eye, taken aback, hangs in irons, canvas cracking
   like cannon — heads near due south, yawing — her leeway
   great."]

Notes, in the same hand:

She gains nothing, if she does not even lose. If I dared sit up
& paddle, I could overhaul her. The water breaker beside the
fore companion — I am sore distress'd for water.

— J.H., in the coracle
