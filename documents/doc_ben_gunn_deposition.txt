DEPOSITION of Benjamin Gunn, mariner, taken at the stockade
this day by D. Livesey.

The deponent saith: That three years agone he sailed in another ship,
which sighted this island. That the deponent, having been in Flint's
ship the Walrus when the treasure was buried, proposed to his messmates
that they should land and seek it. That the captain was displeased, but
the men were all of a mind and went ashore.

That twelve days they sought, and every day had the worse word for the
deponent, until one fine morning all hands went aboard, and left him
with these words: "As for you, Benjamin Gunn," says they, "here's a
musket, and a spade, and pick-axe. You can stay here and find Flint's
money for yourself."

That since that day he hath lived on goats and berries and oysters, and
not a bite of Christian diet. That he is now desirous to quit the island,
and will in return render such service as is in his power; expecting
from the gentlemen a passage home, and such reward as they shall think
fit — the deponent naming the sum of one thousand pounds.

Taken down in his presence, and read over to him.

D. Livesey
