[From among the HISPANIOLA's papers — a much-thumbed
manuscript primer, salt-stained at the corners, kept in
the cabin for the breaking-in of green hands. In the
Captain's clerkly hand.]

A SHORT ACCOUNT
of the Parts of a Schooner, & their Uses;
for Lads going first to Sea.

The KEEL is the backbone of the ship, the great timber
along her bottom; all else is built upon it. The HULL
is the body she swims in. The BOWSPRIT is the spar
thrust out over her bows, to spread sail forward.

Her MASTS — fore and main in a schooner — carry the
YARDS and GAFFS, the spars across and abaft them, from
which the SAILS are hung. Sails are the ship's wings;
well trimmed, they draw her through the water; ill
trimmed, they flap and chafe and do nothing.

The RIGGING is all her ropes: the standing rigging
holds the masts up, the running rigging works the
sails. Learn the name of every rope you lay hand to,
or keep your hands off it.

The ANCHOR holds her to the ground; the CAPSTAN, with
its bars, heaves the anchor up and warps her in.
The HELM steers her; the man at the helm minds the
compass and nothing else. The JOLLY-BOAT is the
smallest boat, for running errands between ship
and shore.

RANKS & DUTIES. The CAPTAIN commands all. The MATE
is his right hand. The BOATSWAIN sees the rigging
kept right. The COOK feeds all hands — make him
your friend. Before the mast, every man keeps his
watch, does his duty, and asks no questions above
his station. Mind this, and you will do.

A.S.
