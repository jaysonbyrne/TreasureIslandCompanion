     To the Collector of His Majesty's Customs, Bristol.

     SIR,

     In obedience to intelligence received of a lugger
     lying in Kitt's Hole, I rode out last night with four
     officers in the direction of Black Hill Cove.

     Upon the road, in bright moonlight, we overtook and,
     I regret to report, rode down a blind beggar, who
     thrust himself under the horses' feet.  He was killed
     upon the spot, the four hoofs trampling him.  The
     lad Hawkins, of the Admiral Benbow, was witness, and
     will depose that the man ran wilfully into his death.

     Proceeding to Kitt's Hole, my men dismounted and
     groped down the dingle, leading their horses.  The
     lugger was already under way, though still close in.
     I hailed her; a voice bade me keep out of the
     moonlight, or I should get some lead in me, and a
     bullet whistled close by my arm.  She doubled the
     point and disappeared.

     I have dispatched a man to B---- to warn the cutter,
     which is just about as good as nothing.  They have
     got off clean, and there is an end.

     I am, Sir, your most obedient humble servant,

                                        D. DANCE, Supervisor.

     P.S.  I am glad I trod on Master Pew's corns, for the
     beggar was no beggar, but a ringleader of the gang
     that sacked the Admiral Benbow this same night.
