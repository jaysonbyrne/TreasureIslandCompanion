     [A page from Jim Hawkins's sea journal, kept in a
      copy-book stitched with twine.]

     At sea, 12 days out.  Wind fair, W.S.W.  Course S.
     by W.  Ship logs 7 knots.  Weather fine and warm.

     Mr. Arrow turned drunk again to-day and fell down the
     companion; the captain ordered him below in disgrace.
     The men laugh at him, which the captain does not like.

     Silver's parrot screams "Pieces of eight!" all day.
     The men call the cook Barbecue, and love him.  He
     tells stories of Flint and England and Davis, and the
     men sit round the galley and listen with their mouths
     open.  I am half in love with him myself, and yet
     there is something in his smile I do not trust.

     I keep to my books when I can, and to the doctor, who
     is teaching me my latitudes.  The captain has the
     true bearings locked in his cabin, and tells no one.

     There is a party aboard, and the cook is the head of
     it -- the cook's party, I call them in my own mind --
     Hands, Merry, Morgan, and the rest, always together,
     always whispering.  Abraham Gray keeps clear of them,
     and so do I.
