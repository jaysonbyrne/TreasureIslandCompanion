Taken down by Jim Hawkins from the talk of Israel Hands, coxswain,
late gunner to Captain Flint, in the North Inlet, a little before
he died.

—

"For thirty years," he said, "I've sail'd the seas and seen good
and bad, better and worse, fair weather and foul, provisions
running out, knives going, and what not. Well, now I tell you,
I never seen good come o' goodness yet. Him as strikes first
is my fancy; dead men don't bite; them's my views — amen,
so be it.

"I was Flint's gunner, I was, when Flint was Flint. There was
a crew! Allardyce, and O'Brien there" — here he look'd upon
the dead man at his feet — "and the rest, as is food for fishes
long ago. We took the gold where we found it, and we buried it
where Flint bid us, and never a man of us know'd the place but
him, and him dead these years. What there is in that cave, lad,
was got with blood, and blood'll have it again, you mark me.

"You can kill the body, but not the spirit — that's what you
say. Howsomever, sperrits don't reckon for much, by what I've
seen. I'll chance it with the sperrits, Jim."

He said no more of consequence, but ask'd for wine, and fell
to his old smiling again.

— J.H.
